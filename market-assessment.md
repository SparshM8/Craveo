# Craveo market assessment

## Executive verdict

**Craveo is a strong product idea, but the broad category is no longer untouched.** I would rate the current concept **7.5/10 as a startup opportunity**: the customer problem is real, the behavior is timely, and the experience could be memorable. I would rate its current **novelty at 5/10**, because major platforms are already testing short-form restaurant video. The opportunity becomes much stronger if Craveo is positioned not as “food delivery plus reels,” but as **the trusted, shoppable local food-video network where every appetizing moment is connected to a real dish that can be ordered now**.

| Dimension | Rating | Assessment |
|---|---:|---|
| Customer problem | 8.5/10 | People often know they are hungry but do not know what they want. Visual food content can reduce uncertainty and create appetite. |
| Product experience | 8.5/10 | The journey from seeing preparation or plating to ordering the same bite is naturally understandable and emotionally powerful. |
| Market timing | 8/10 | Food delivery is mature but still innovating around new occasions, discovery, affordability, and engagement. |
| Differentiation today | 5/10 | Short-form restaurant discovery is already being tested by incumbents, especially Swiggy Bites. [1] |
| Defensibility potential | 7/10 | The moat could come from local creator supply, item-level content-to-order data, trust systems, and restaurant ROI—not from the video feed alone. |
| Execution difficulty | 9/10 difficulty | You would be combining marketplace liquidity, video moderation, recommendations, restaurant operations, payments, delivery reliability, and creator incentives. |
| Overall opportunity | **7.5/10** | Worth building and testing, but only with a sharp wedge and a narrow initial geography. |

## Is the idea already in the market?

**Yes, partially.** The exact Craveo vision is not a completely new category, but the market appears to be validating the direction from multiple angles.

First, MediaNama reported that Swiggy began rolling out **Bites**, a vertical short-video restaurant feed inside Dineout in Delhi NCR, Mumbai, and Bengaluru. The reported experience includes restaurant clips, restaurant metadata, offers, likes, shares, and a direct “Book Now” action. This is a direct signal that a major Indian food platform sees short-form video as a restaurant-discovery surface rather than merely a marketing channel. [1]

Second, Food On Demand reported that CookUnity acquired **flavrs**, a shoppable-video platform for food discovery. The reported product connects videos to real-time menus, nutrition information, and user preferences, allowing viewers to move from inspiration to purchase. This validates the broader video-to-food-commerce behavior outside India as well. [2]

Third, the incumbent scale is substantial. Swiggy reported **15.1 million food-delivery monthly transacting users** and **251.7 thousand monthly transacting restaurant partners** in Q4 FY2025. [3] Eternal’s Q4 FY2026 shareholder letter stated that **109 million Indians completed transactions across Blinkit, District, and Zomato during FY2026**, and that the company’s platforms support more than **400,000 restaurants** and over **1 million delivery partners**. [4] These figures show both the size of the demand and the difficulty of competing with established distribution, trust, logistics, and merchant density.

| Market layer | Existing reality | Craveo implication |
|---|---|---|
| Food delivery | Zomato and Swiggy already own habitual ordering, merchant supply, delivery operations, and customer trust. [3] [4] | Do not try to beat them immediately on delivery coverage or discounts. |
| Restaurant video discovery | Swiggy Bites shows that a major incumbent is already testing a reels-style restaurant surface. [1] | “We have reels” is not a defensible pitch. |
| Shoppable food video | CookUnity’s flavrs acquisition shows that video can be tied to menus and purchase intent. [2] | The video-to-order loop is commercially credible, but execution and niche matter. |
| Customer-created food content | Customers already create food reviews, reactions, and recommendations on social platforms. | Craveo can organize that content around local availability, dish identity, and trust. |
| Local restaurant economics | Independent restaurants need visibility but may struggle to produce professional content or understand what converts. | A simple creator toolkit and measurable order attribution could be a strong supply-side wedge. |

## What could make Craveo genuinely different?

Craveo should own the idea of **“watchable intent.”** A normal food-delivery search starts with a user who already has a dish or cuisine in mind. A social feed starts with attention but often ends without a reliable local purchase. Craveo can sit between the two: the user discovers a craving visually, and the product immediately resolves it into a real, nearby, orderable item.

The core product primitive should be a **Bite Card**, not a generic reel. Each video should be attached to a specific restaurant, dish, price, serving size, dietary information, delivery radius, current availability, and source label. The key button should be **Order this bite**, not a vague “visit profile.” When the dish is unavailable, Craveo should offer the closest live alternative instead of sending the user to a dead page.

A second differentiator should be **trust by design**. Every reel should clearly identify whether it was posted by the restaurant, a verified customer, a paid creator, or Craveo editorial. Customer reviews should be linked to actual orders where possible. The platform should expose when a dish was filmed, whether the price has changed, and whether the featured item is currently available. MediaNama’s reporting on Swiggy Bites specifically highlights open questions around upload eligibility, moderation, ranking, and disclosure. [1] Craveo can turn those weaknesses into a product promise.

A third differentiator should be **restaurant ROI**. Restaurants should see which reel generated views, menu opens, add-to-carts, completed orders, repeat orders, and saves. A restaurant should be able to upload one short video, tag a dish, and understand whether the content generated business. This is more valuable than vanity metrics and gives restaurants a reason to keep supplying content.

## The “crazy” product direction

The most compelling version of Craveo is a **live visual food map**. The user can swipe through nearby dishes, but the feed is constrained by reality: open restaurants, delivery radius, current menu availability, preparation time, price, and dietary preferences. The content feels social, but the underlying system behaves like commerce.

A future Craveo experience could include a “Crave Mode” prompt such as “I want something crispy, spicy, under ₹400, delivered in 30 minutes.” The system would return a short sequence of videos rather than a static list. The user could tap a reaction—spicy, creamy, crunchy, comfort, healthy, late-night—and the feed would adapt. A group of friends could react to the same reel and create a shared cart. A restaurant could post a limited “fresh batch” reel tied to a quantity or time window. A customer could post a reaction that becomes shoppable only after the dish, restaurant, and order are verified.

The perspective change is important: **Craveo does not ask only “Which restaurant do you want?” It asks “What food moment do you want right now?”** That shift can make food ordering more emotional, exploratory, and culturally rich.

## Biggest risks

The first risk is **incumbent imitation**. Swiggy or Zomato can copy a reels interface quickly and distribute it to a much larger existing customer base. Craveo therefore needs a supply, trust, or data advantage that is difficult to copy, such as the best verified customer food-video network in a specific city or the best restaurant content-to-order analytics.

The second risk is **content liquidity**. A feed with weak, repetitive, or outdated videos will feel worse than a normal restaurant listing. Craveo should launch with a narrow city zone and guarantee enough quality content before expanding.

The third risk is **visual deception**. Food can look excellent on video while arriving poorly, being unavailable, or costing more than expected. Availability, price, freshness, source labels, and post-order feedback must be visible and reliable.

The fourth risk is **economics**. Video creation, moderation, creator incentives, payment processing, delivery support, and restaurant acquisition all cost money. A video view is not valuable unless it improves menu opens, conversion, average order value, repeat purchase, or restaurant retention.

The fifth risk is **trying to build all of Zomato too early**. Logistics, refunds, customer support, payments, menu management, and delivery operations are difficult businesses. Craveo should first prove that visual discovery increases order conversion and restaurant demand before investing in a full delivery network.

## Recommended launch wedge

Start with **one dense neighborhood in Bengaluru**, where the current Craveo prototype is already visually themed, and recruit 50–100 restaurants across a few high-video categories such as biryani, burgers, desserts, cafés, street food, and regional specialties. Seed the product with restaurant content and a small number of paid or invited local food creators. The first experience should include a full-screen reel feed, item-linked bite cards, restaurant pages, a menu/cart handoff, saves, likes, reviews, and a simple “order this bite” conversion path.

For the first launch, do not promise nationwide delivery. Test the discovery and conversion loop with restaurant-direct ordering, an existing ordering partner, or a controlled local operations model. The strategic question is whether Craveo can create **incremental orders and better discovery** before it attempts to own every delivery kilometer.

| Phase | Product proof to establish | Success signal |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Content and discovery | People watch, save, share, and open restaurant pages from reels. | Strong completion rate and reel-to-restaurant click-through. |
| 2. Shoppable bites | Viewers add the featured dish to a cart. | Reel-to-cart conversion materially better than static listing traffic. |
| 3. Restaurant ROI | Restaurants can see which content drives business. | Restaurants upload repeatedly and retain customers from Craveo. |
| 4. Local commerce | Orders, payment, fulfilment, refunds, and reviews work reliably in one zone. | Repeat order rate and contribution margin become measurable. |
| 5. Network expansion | Content and demand compound across neighborhoods and cities. | New restaurants and creators join because the network already produces demand. |

## Metrics that decide whether the idea is working

The most important metric is not daily active users. It is **completed orders per 1,000 qualified reel views**, split by restaurant reel, customer reel, and Craveo editorial reel. Track reel completion, restaurant-page opens, dish-page opens, add-to-cart rate, checkout conversion, order cancellation, review rate, repeat order within 30 days, restaurant content reuse, and creator earnings or incentives.

A strong early signal would be that users who discover a restaurant through a reel order or save at a meaningfully higher rate than users who arrive through a static listing. A strong supply signal would be restaurants uploading new content without being repeatedly paid for every post. A strong defensibility signal would be that Craveo’s local content and conversion data improve recommendations in a way a copied interface cannot immediately reproduce.

## Final rating

**My honest rating is 7.5/10 today, with an 8.5–9/10 upside if the execution is sharply focused.** The idea is real, market-relevant, and capable of changing how customers choose food. It is not unique merely because it combines delivery and reels; the market is already moving in that direction. The winning version is a **trusted, local, orderable visual food network** where every video is fresh, attributable, connected to a real dish, and measured by actual customer satisfaction and completed orders.

The next build should therefore focus on one exceptional loop: **watch a real local food moment → understand what it is → trust it → order it → review it → make the next person confident.**
